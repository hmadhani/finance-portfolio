## How options are priced (foundational papers)

- **Black, F., & Scholes, M. (1973). "The Pricing of Options and Corporate Liabilities." *Journal of Political Economy* 81(3), 637–654.** Metadata verified directly on journals.uchicago.edu and via the Crossref API. Landing page verified; summary from abstract not available. https://doi.org/10.1086/260062 · verified 2026-09-27
  - *Where it shows up in this portfolio:* `scripts/optlib/bs.py` (`bs_price`, `bs_greeks`) is a direct implementation of the closed-form model this paper introduces (European exercise, no dividends).
- **Merton, R. C. (1973). "Theory of Rational Option Pricing." *The Bell Journal of Economics and Management Science* 4(1), 141–183.** (Journal name corrected from the candidate table's "Bell J. Econ." to the full title on record with Crossref.) Metadata verified via the Crossref API. Summary (read on the EconPapers/RePEc listing page for this article): derives option-pricing restrictions from the assumption that investors prefer more wealth to less, extending the Black-Scholes approach to warrants and down-and-out options. https://doi.org/10.2307/3003143 · verified 2026-09-27
  - *Where it shows up in this portfolio:* background reading on the option-pricing theory that the Black-Scholes formula in `scripts/optlib/bs.py` is a special case of; this toolkit does not implement Merton's dividend/early-exercise extensions.
- **Cox, J. C., Ross, S. A., & Rubinstein, M. (1979). "Option Pricing: A Simplified Approach." *Journal of Financial Economics* 7(3), 229–263.** Metadata verified via the Crossref API. Landing page verified (Elsevier redirected to a page that did not render for automated fetch); summary from abstract not available. https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(79)90015-1 · verified 2026-09-27
  - *Where it shows up in this portfolio:* background on the discrete-time binomial approach as an alternative derivation of the same option-pricing theory; this toolkit uses the closed-form Black-Scholes model directly (`scripts/optlib/bs.py`), not a binomial tree.
- **Heston, S. L. (1993). "A Closed-Form Solution for Options with Stochastic Volatility with Applications to Bond and Currency Options." *The Review of Financial Studies* 6(2), 327–343.** Metadata verified on academic.oup.com and via the Crossref API. Landing page verified; summary from abstract not available. https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/6.2.327 · verified 2026-09-27
  - *Where it shows up in this portfolio:* `scripts/optlib/bs.py` takes a single constant `sigma` per pricing call; Heston's model makes volatility itself stochastic, which this toolkit does not attempt.

## What the evidence says about option returns and selling premium

- **Coval, J. D., & Shumway, T. (2001). "Expected Option Returns." *The Journal of Finance* 56(3), 983–1009.** Finds call/put option returns depart from CAPM predictions (rising with strike, and zero-beta straddles losing money on average), pointing to a priced factor such as stochastic volatility. https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00352 · verified 2026-09-27
  - *Where it shows up in this portfolio:* motivates selling (rather than buying) rich premium — short-volatility positions have historically earned a return.
- **Bakshi, G., & Kapadia, N. (2003). "Delta-Hedged Gains and the Negative Market Volatility Risk Premium." *The Review of Financial Studies* 16(2), 527–566.** Read from the authors' own posted copy of the article. Finds delta-hedged S&P 500 index option portfolios systematically underperform zero, more so in high-volatility periods, supporting a negative market volatility risk premium. https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhg002 · verified 2026-09-27
  - *Where it shows up in this portfolio:* the volatility risk premium is the economic rationale for the profile's rule of selling premium when IV rank is high.
- **Carr, P., & Wu, L. (2009). "Variance Risk Premiums." *The Review of Financial Studies* 22(3), 1311–1341.** Read on the RePEc/IDEAS listing page for this article. Proposes measuring the variance risk premium as the gap between realized variance and a synthetic variance-swap rate built from a portfolio of options, and studies it across five indexes and 35 stocks. https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhn038 · verified 2026-09-27
  - *Where it shows up in this portfolio:* underpins the variance/volatility-risk-premium framing used to decide when to sell premium.
- **Goyal, A., & Saretto, A. (2009). "Cross-Section of Option Returns and Volatility." *Journal of Financial Economics* 94(2), 310–326.** Metadata verified via the Crossref API and the Semantic Scholar API (both list Amit Goyal and Alessio Saretto as authors); summary from abstract not available — both APIs report the abstract withheld by the publisher (ScienceDirect requires a subscription). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2009.01.001 · verified 2026-09-27
  - *Where it shows up in this portfolio:* background for the IV-rank entry thresholds in `options/investor-profile.md`.
- **Whaley, R. E. (2002). "Return and Risk of CBOE Buy Write Monthly Index." *The Journal of Derivatives* 10(2), 35–42.** Read via the Wayback Machine's 2022 archive of the journal's abstract page (the live page sits behind a login wall). Introduces the CBOE's BXM index and finds the underlying covered-call strategy earned nearly as much as the S&P 500 from 1988–2001 with substantially lower risk. https://doi.org/10.3905/jod.2002.319194 · verified 2026-09-27
  - *Where it shows up in this portfolio:* the original research behind the BXM benchmark used in this portfolio.
- **Israelov, R., & Nielsen, L. N. (2015). "Covered Calls Uncovered." *Financial Analysts Journal* 71(6), 44–57.** Correction: the candidate table's DOI (`10.2469/faj.v71.n6.4`) resolves to a different article ("Liquid Betting against Beta in Dow Jones Industrial Average Stocks," Auer & Schuhmacher, pp. 30–43, confirmed via the Crossref API); the correct DOI, confirmed the same way and by reading the article's own text (the authors' AQR-hosted PDF), is `10.2469/faj.v71.n6.1`. Decomposes covered-call returns into equity, short-volatility, and an uncompensated equity-reversal exposure, finding equity exposure drives most of the risk and return. https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v71.n6.1 · verified 2026-09-27
  - *Where it shows up in this portfolio:* caution that a covered-call/short-premium strategy still carries most of its risk from the underlying equity exposure, not from the short option itself.
